## Notes

### How they apply for jobs today
- How long searching, applications in total: during the 1st and 2nd year of study, about two years, not all the time; about 10 applications, "not more".
- Channels / job boards: mostly Djinni.
- What they send: a CV and a GitHub link. Sometimes a questionnaire from the company, not a skill task.
- Feedback they get from employers: either no reply at all or a quick, standard rejection ("Sorry, you are not a fit"), with no reason.
- Where the process usually stops: before or right after the first HR conversation. The last application was for a backend trainee role; they do not remember the company.

### Proof of skills
- What they count as proof: not answered directly. Asked whether job ads required proof of a skill: no, at that time they did not.
- Evidence that an employer looked at it: not sure anyone opened their GitHub, because they never got any reaction to it.
- Asked to prove a skill: no. The only interview was a conversation with HR; nobody checked a specific skill.

### Numbers (baseline)
- Applications per week: not asked; about 10 in two years in total.
- Interview invitations per 10 applications: 1 (a trainee role, HR only, did not fit them).
- Source: self-reported

- What would stop them: HR ignoring it.
- First skill they want a report for: not asked.

## [our note] What this tells us
- **Supports PRODUCT.md:** generic rejections with no reason; GitHub gets no visible reaction; the only interview did not check a skill.
- **Challenges PRODUCT.md:** the first filter is HR, and the client expects HR to ignore a skill report. This weakens red-team assumption 3 (a report gets a CV past the screen). We still have no conversation with anyone who hires juniors.
- **Volume:** about 10 applications in two years is very few. Part of the problem may be how little they applied, not only missing proof.

## [our note] What changes for the next interview
- Name the result "a skill report you can share", the same words as PRODUCT.md, not "certificate".
- Ask question 6 word for word: "What do you count as proof?"
- Ask "Who read your application first, HR or a technical person? How do you know?"
- Ask questions 14 and 15 before time runs out.
